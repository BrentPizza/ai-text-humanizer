# APA 7th Edition Quick Reference

## Page setup
- Margins 1" all sides; US Letter (in docx: 12240 × 15840 DXA, 1440 DXA margins)
- Font: one of Times New Roman 12, Calibri 11, Arial 11, Georgia 11, Lucida Sans Unicode 10
- Double-spaced throughout, including title page and references; no extra space between paragraphs
- First-line indent 0.5" for body paragraphs; left-aligned, ragged right (no full justification)
- Page numbers top right on every page, starting at title page (page 1)
- Running head: professional papers only (ALL CAPS short title, flush left, ≤50 characters)

## Title page (opt-in only)
Default: no separate title page; the bold centered title sits at the top of page 1 above the first paragraph.
**Student:** title (bold, centered, title case, 3–4 double-spaced lines down from the top margin), one blank double-spaced line, then author, affiliation (department, university), course number and name, instructor, due date, each on its own centered line in plain text. Page number top right; no running head. The text starts on page 2 with the title repeated in bold, centered, followed directly by the first paragraph.
**Professional:** title, author byline, affiliation, author note (ORCID, disclosures, correspondence). Running head on all pages.

## Abstract (professional, or if required)
"Abstract" bold centered; one non-indented paragraph, 150–250 words; then indented *Keywords:* italic label followed by 3–5 keywords.

## Headings (opt-in only)
Default for this skill: **no section headings.** Title, continuous paragraphs, references. APA 7 allows headings but does not require them, and the user's papers don't use them. Use the table below only when the user asks for headings.

| Level | Format |
|---|---|
| 1 | Centered, Bold, Title Case |
| 2 | Flush Left, Bold, Title Case |
| 3 | Flush Left, Bold Italic, Title Case |
| 4 | Indented, Bold, Title Case, Ending With a Period. Text begins on same line. |
| 5 | Indented, Bold Italic, Title Case, Ending With a Period. Text begins on same line. |

Paper title repeats (bold, centered) on the first page of text. The intro gets no "Introduction" heading.

## In-text citations
| Authors | Parenthetical | Narrative |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | (Smith, 2020) | Smith (2020) |
| 2 | (Smith & Lee, 2020) | Smith and Lee (2020) |
| 3+ | (Smith et al., 2020) | Smith et al. (2020) |
| Group w/ abbreviation | first: (American Psychological Association [APA], 2020); then (APA, 2020) | |
| No date | (Smith, n.d.) | |
| Multiple works | (Lee, 2019; Smith, 2020), alphabetical | |
| Same author, same year | (Smith, 2020a, 2020b) | |

Direct quotes: (Smith, 2020, p. 14) or (Smith, 2020, para. 3). Quotes of 40+ words become block quotes: indented 0.5", no quotation marks, citation after the final punctuation.

## Reference list
- New page, "References" bold centered
- Hanging indent 0.5", double-spaced, alphabetical by first author surname
- Up to 20 authors listed; for 21+, first 19, ellipsis, last author
- DOI as URL: https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx (no "Retrieved from", no period after DOI/URL)

### Patterns
**Journal article**
Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article in sentence case. *Journal Name in Title Case, Volume*(Issue), pages. https://doi.org/xx

**Book**
Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of book in sentence case* (2nd ed.). Publisher. https://doi.org/xx

**Edited book chapter**
Author, A. A. (Year). Title of chapter. In E. E. Editor (Ed.), *Title of book* (pp. xx–xx). Publisher.

**Webpage**
Author, A. A., or Group. (Year, Month Day). *Title of page*. Site Name. URL
(Omit Site Name if same as author. Add "Retrieved Month Day, Year, from URL" only for content designed to change.)

**Government / technical report**
Agency Name. (Year). *Title of report* (Report No. xxx). Publisher if different. URL

**Generative AI output (if disclosure required)**
OpenAI. (2025). *ChatGPT* (Version) [Large language model]. https://chat.openai.com/
Anthropic. (2026). *Claude* (Version) [Large language model]. https://claude.ai/

## Mechanics
- Serial (Oxford) comma
- One space after periods
- Numbers: words for zero–nine, numerals for 10+; always numerals for statistics, percentages, measurements, times, ages, money, and numbers in the abstract
- Seriation in sentences: (a), (b), (c)
- Italicize key terms on first use, statistical symbols, book/journal titles, and anchors of scales
- Abbreviations defined at first use; don't abbreviate in headings unless defined
